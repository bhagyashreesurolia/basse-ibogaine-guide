What Is Ibogaine and How Does It Work?

Derived from the root bark of Tabernanthe iboga, a shrub native to Central West Africa, ibogaine's ability to interrupt acute opioid withdrawal was discovered in 1962 by Howard Lotsof. In modern clinical practice, ibogaine modulates opioid receptors to rapidly reduce withdrawal discomfort while promoting neuroplasticity—helping the brain form new neural pathways.

Clinical evidence cited by Bassé includes a 2018 study of problematic opioid use where 80% of participants reported their withdrawal was eliminated or drastically reduced, alongside a 2021 systematic review demonstrating reduced cravings and withdrawal symptoms. Understanding the difference between Iboga and Ibogaine is essential: ceremonial Iboga contains 12 alkaloids, whereas clinical programs administer purified Ibogaine HCl so the liver converts it into its active metabolite, noribogaine.
